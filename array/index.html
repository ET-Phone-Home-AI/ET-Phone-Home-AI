Pattern multiplication, seen live

Turn the element down to isotropic and the total collapses onto the array factor. Turn it back to a dipole and the null along the axis carves the endfire lobe away. The total is always the product, point by point.

Where grating lobes come from

Push d past half a wavelength while scanning off broadside and a second full-strength lobe walks in from the horizon. The condition is d/λ < 1/(1+|cos θ0|) — the tool flags it as it happens.

The taper trade

Binomial weighting erases the sidelobes entirely and pays for it in beamwidth and directivity. Uniform gives the narrowest beam and −13.2 dB sidelobes. Everything useful lives between the two.

The 2D cut is a slice of a 3D shape

Because the array and its dipoles share one axis, the pattern never depends on φ — the whole lobe is just this elevation cut spun around the array axis. Broadside sweeps out a doughnut around the array; endfire collapses that doughnut into a single cone hugging the axis. Scan between them in the 3D view and watch it happen.
