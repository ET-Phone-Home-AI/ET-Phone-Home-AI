What a and b each set

The dominant TE10 cutoff depends only on the broad wall: half a wavelength has to fit across a. Slide b and the TE10 cutoff does not move — but b sets the TE01 cutoff, so once b > a/2 it is TE01, not TE20, that ends the single-mode band. The narrow wall also sets power handling and conductor loss.

Below cutoff is not reflection

Under fc the mode still exists, but β turns imaginary and the field decays as e−αz without carrying power. That evanescent tail is exactly what a waveguide-below-cutoff attenuator sells.

The usable band is narrow on purpose

Standard guides are specified from roughly 1.25 fc to 1.9 fc: far enough above cutoff that dispersion and loss are tame, far enough below the next mode that any higher mode a discontinuity excites stays evanescent and cannot carry power away.

Assumptions

Ideal, perfectly conducting walls for the modes and fields; the wall-loss readout is the TE10 conductor loss for copper (σ = 5.8×107 S/m) and applies to TE10 only. The fill is a lossless, non-magnetic dielectric. Fields are normalized spatial patterns: the longitudinal colour map and the transverse arrows are scaled independently, so their relative sizes are not physical E/H amplitude ratios; the colour map and the arrows are 90° apart in time, and absolute amplitude is not shown.
