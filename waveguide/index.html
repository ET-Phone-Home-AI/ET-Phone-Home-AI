Why a sets everything

The dominant TE10 cutoff depends only on the broad wall: half a wavelength has to fit across a. Slide b and watch the cutoff refuse to move — b buys you power handling and lower loss, not bandwidth.

Below cutoff is not reflection

Under fc the mode still exists, but β turns imaginary and the field decays as e−αz without carrying power. That evanescent tail is exactly what a waveguide-below-cutoff attenuator sells.

The usable band is narrow on purpose

Standard guides are specified from roughly 1.25 fc to 1.9 fc: far enough above cutoff that dispersion and loss are tame, far enough below the next mode that a discontinuity cannot excite it.
